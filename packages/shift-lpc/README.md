# @audio/shift-lpc [![npm](https://img.shields.io/npm/v/@audio/shift-lpc)](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@audio/shift-lpc) [![MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/MIT-%E0%A5%90-white)](https://github.com/krishnized/license)

LPC source-filter pitch shift — repitched excitation residual through the unmodified all-pole vocal-tract filter

```
npm install @audio/shift-lpc
```

```js
import lpc from '@audio/shift-lpc'
```

Canonical LPC source-filter pitch shift (residual-excited linear prediction, RELP lineage). Per frame: fit the vocal-tract all-pole filter A(z) by the autocorrelation method (Levinson-Durbin), inverse-filter the signal down to its spectrally-flat excitation residual, repitch that residual with the `delay` line splicer, then resynthesize through the **unmodified** 1/A(z) run continuously with block-switched coefficients. Over the take the synthesis filter's poles never move — the classical speech-processing complement to `formant`'s cepstral envelope preservation, built on a different mechanism (an explicit source-filter model instead of a magnitude-envelope correction).

```js
lpc(audio, { ratio: 1.5 })
lpc(audio, { ratio: 1.5, order: 24, frameSize: 512 })
```

| Param | Default | |
|---|---|---|
| `order` | `min(frameSize/16, round(2 + sr/1000))` | AR filter order (pole count) |
| `frameSize` | `1024` | Analysis/synthesis frame length |

**Preserves** formant frequencies by construction — the filter that carries them is never touched, only its excitation is repitched.<br>
**Destroys** pure tones by design: on a single sinusoid the AR envelope IS the partial, so the fit notches it out of the residual: what is left of it shifts, at the tone's level only where the tone is low or the shift small, and its onset rings at the original pitch, the family's defining tradeoff, not a bug. The level match keeps the take at the input's level, so what the body loses, the onset's ring carries, but it raises a render by 20 dB at most: a tone whose match asks more takes 20 dB and comes out under its input by the rest, 5 of 48 tones at ±5 semitones by up to 2.7 dB and 18 of 48 at ±12 by up to 4.7 dB (250 Hz to 4 kHz, 22.05 to 96 kHz). At 44.1 kHz a 110 Hz tone's body keeps its level within 1 dB at every shift to 12 semitones either way, 220 Hz to five semitones and at −12 (11 dB under at +12); 440 Hz lies up to 5 dB under to five semitones and at −12 (24 dB at +12), 880 Hz 8 to 13 dB under at five semitones and 23 to 38 dB at twelve, 1760 Hz 32 to 49 dB and 3520 Hz 47 to 52 dB at five and twelve (`node scripts/ends.js lpc`).

Formant dist 1.400 is higher (worse) than `formant`'s 0.760 on this synthetic vowel too, across every `order` from 8 to 64: the fitted envelope follows the fixture's individual harmonics as well as its three formants, where `formant`'s deliberately-smoothed cepstral envelope doesn't. None of this is the intended material: real, less strictly periodic speech is what an unmodified synthesis filter is for.

lpc renders a call as one take, whatever it holds: no level is silence, and nothing parts it. Each frame of a quarter `frameSize` takes the fit of its forward window, the fit window that starts with it, unless another window that holds it whitens its own samples by 0.5 dB more. A held tone's frames gain at most 0.31 dB from another window and a held reed-like note's nothing, so they keep their forward windows and their fit does not step from window to window; a held vowel's frames gain up to 2.01 dB, and 30 % of them take another window; where a take is cut into quieter sound, the frames before the cut, whose forward windows cross it, gain 13.5 dB at the median from a window of the take and take that one, and those after it take fits of what follows; the voice's frames gain 0.65 dB at the median (`node scripts/ends.js margin`). Where the past predicts what is no longer there, as at a cut, the inverse filter's memory is cleared and the frame's samples on either side take fits of their own; the filter rings on over the cut, as at a stop. A memory is cleared only where that saves the frame at least the share of its residual that the `order` samples it changes hold, so a held note, which its past predicts, clears none inside it: a reed-like note with steep edges, at 22.05 to 96 kHz and ±5 and ±12 semitones, keeps the loudest 2 ms of its second difference within 6.5 dB of its median, nothing clicking, and the energy between its harmonics 26.1 dB under that on them at least (`node scripts/ends.js held`). Every fit is bandwidth-expanded, a_k·0.994^k (the weighting factor of FED-STD-1016 § 3.7.2), so no pole sits on the unit circle and a pure tone renders bounded: over the take's middle at most 1.06 times its peak at ±5 semitones and 0.98 times at ±12, over its first 4096 frames, where the onset rings the fit's pole, at most 9.82 and 13.66 times (`node scripts/ends.js lpc`: 250 Hz to 4 kHz, 22.05 to 96 kHz). A tone moved past half the rate, which the shift leaves silent, stays 85.9 dB under its input over its middle at least, but its onset rings at the tone's own pitch, and its match asks more than 20 dB to bring that ring to the input's energy: it takes 20, the take comes out 2.5 to 7.8 dB under its input, and the ring's peak reaches 17.7 dB over the input's (`node scripts/ends.js match`). After 0.25 s of silence a tone's match asks more: the click, which can land up to 20 ms before the tone, reaches 9.38 times the tone's peak at ±5 semitones and 6.72 times at ±12, and 18 and 36 of the 48 tones come out under their input, by up to 18.7 and 28.8 dB (`node scripts/ends.js lpc`); a tone moved past half the rate comes out 25.0 to 29.5 dB under its input there, its peak 12.8 dB over the input's (`node scripts/ends.js match`).

Every level lpc compares is the call's own, a ratio of two energies or gains, none an absolute level, so a call times a factor renders as its render times that factor. A power of two does so bit for bit but where a ring's far tail falls into float32's subnormals, under 2^-126, and rounds otherwise: no sample over 2^-118.7 differs, none within 569.7 dB of the render's peak. Any other factor does so within its rounding, on the voice to −94.3 dB re the peak; on a pure tone a rounding can move the delay line's splices, to −13.8 dB (`node scripts/ends.js scale`: 675 channel renders, the voice and two tones at 22.05 to 96 kHz, alone and beside a channel 30 dB down, 2^-20 to 2^4).

**Use when:** Speech/vocals where the formants must not move with pitch, and the material isn't a bare sustained tone.<br>
**Not for:** Pure tones, synth pads, or anything where the "partial" and the "envelope" are the same thing.

## Stream

```js
let write = lpc({ ratio: 1.5 })
let out = write(inputBlock)
let tail = write()  // flush
```

`lpc` buffers the whole input; `write(chunk)` accumulates and the batch algorithm runs once on `write()` (flush, no argument), returning everything at once.

## Data

Input is a `Float32Array` (mono) or an array of `Float32Array` channels (`[left, right, ...]`) — anything else throws `TypeError`. `ratio` also accepts a function `t => ratio` (seconds from stream start) or a `Float32Array` breakpoint envelope (resampled across the input via `ratioDuration`, default the input's own duration) for time-varying pitch.
